# VCH AI Usage Level 1 – Aware Chat User

*Syllabus / Teacher Documentation*

---

## 1. Module Overview

**Title:** AI Usage Level 1 – Aware Chat User
**Context:** Value Chain Hackers (VCH) – Supply chain finance & sustainability projects
**Position in AI Ladder:** Entry level (before Level 2 – Source Collector and Curator)

This syllabus defines what it means for a student team to work at **AI Level 1 – Aware Chat User**, how this compares to:

* **Students who do not use AI at all**, and
* **“Normal” AI students** (unstructured ChatGPT usage),

and how teachers can **teach, coach, and assess** this level within the VCH programme.

The core shift at Level 1:

> From “AI says so” or “I don’t use AI”
> to
> **“AI helps me understand and find real sources, but I always verify.”**

---

## 2. Baseline: Student Who Does Not Use AI At All

### 2.1 Typical behaviour

A student who does not use AI at all:

* Relies on:

  * Google/Bing search,
  * Random websites and PDFs,
  * Textbooks and lecture slides.
* Often:

  * Scans the first 2–3 search results,
  * Reads quickly, copies key sentences,
  * Pastes text into Word or PowerPoint with minimal adaptation.
* May:

  * Struggle to understand complex English texts,
  * Have difficulty summarising or structuring information,
  * Spend a lot of time on basic reading and note-taking.

### 2.2 Strengths

* Less risk of blindly trusting hallucinated AI content.
* Direct contact with primary texts (if they actually read them).
* Intuitive sense of “this looks like an official website vs a random blog”.

### 2.3 Limitations

* Time-consuming reading and summarising tasks.
* Lower throughput: they review fewer documents and perspectives.
* May avoid complex sources because they feel “too hard”.
* No explicit reflection on:

  * “Where does this information come from?”
  * “How could a tool help me understand this better?”

### 2.4 Why Level 1 is an upgrade

Level 1 does not replace reading or critical thinking.
It adds:

* **Faster understanding** (AI explanations in simple language),
* **Better coverage** (more sources scanned),
* **Structured reflection** on:

  * How AI was used,
  * Where AI was wrong or shallow,
  * How real sources were used to correct it.

The **non-AI student** and the **Level 1 student** can reach similar truths,
but the Level 1 student can do so **faster and more transparently**.

## 3. Comparison: Non-AI vs “Normal AI” vs Level 1

### 3.1 Non-AI student

* Tools:

  * Browser search, Word, PowerPoint, PDFs.
* Behaviour:

  * Reads and copy-pastes text.
* Risk:

  * Limited scope, slow, but fewer hallucinations.

### 3.2 “Normal AI” student (unstructured)

* Tools:

  * Public ChatGPT or similar, in the browser.
* Behaviour:

  * Asks generic questions,
  * Copies AI answers into assignments,
  * Believes confident text = correct text.
* Risk:

  * High hallucination risk,
  * No clear sources, no transparency.

### 3.3 Level 1 – Aware Chat User (VCH standard)

* Tools:

  * **OpenWebUI / ChatGPT (OpenAI stack)** for explanations & rewriting.
  * **SearXNG** for meta search and real sources.
  * **OneDrive / SharePoint / Teams** for storing documents and notes.
* Behaviour:

  * Uses AI to understand and structure content.
  * Uses SearXNG to find and save **real PDFs and websites**.
  * Documents AI usage and sources.
* Risk control:

  * Always checks key AI outputs against at least 1–2 real sources.
  * Keeps a short “AI usage + sources” section in the report.

**USP vs non-AI student:**

* Faster access to explanations and language support.
* Processes more sources in the same time.

**USP vs “normal AI” student:**

* Does **not** blindly trust AI.
* Can show where information comes from and how it was checked.

## 4. Learning Outcomes (Level 1)

After completing Level 1, students will be able to:

1. **Use AI to understand concepts**

   * Use OpenWebUI / ChatGPT to obtain clear explanations of:

     * Theory U,
     * Supply Chain Finance (SCF),
     * CSRD, EUDR, and other key regulations,
     * Core concepts in their specific project.

2. **Find and store real sources**

   * Use SearXNG to locate:

     * At least 3–5 relevant, credible documents per core topic,
     * With a focus on official, NGO, and company sources.
   * Store these in a structured folder system in OneDrive / SharePoint / Teams.

3. **Improve writing with AI**

   * Use AI to:

     * Clean up grammar and spelling,
     * Improve clarity and flow of paragraphs,
     * Suggest basic structure for sections (headings, bullet points).

4. **Document and reflect on AI usage**

   * Add a short **“AI usage and sources”** section in their project report.
   * Provide at least one example of:

     * AI being wrong, shallow, or incomplete,
     * How they corrected it with real sources.

5. **Demonstrate basic truth-seeking behaviour**

   * Treat AI outputs as hypotheses, not facts.
   * Check key claims against **at least 1–2 primary or authoritative sources**.

## 5. Tools & Environment

### 5.1 Required tools for students

* **OpenWebUI / ChatGPT (OpenAI stack)**

  * Interface: browser or integrated tool.
  * Use: concept explanations, summaries, rewrites.

* **SearXNG meta search**

  * Use: finding real documents (PDFs, official sites, NGO reports).

* **Microsoft 365**

  * **OneDrive / SharePoint**

    * Folder structures per project:

      * `ProjectName/01-Sources/Topic/`
  * **Teams / OneNote / Word / PowerPoint**

    * To keep notes, drafts, and final reports.

### 5.2 Recommended structures

* Folder example on OneDrive / SharePoint:

  * `ProjectName/`

    * `01-Sources/`

      * `Theory-U/`
      * `SCF/`
      * `CSRD/`
    * `02-Notes/`
    * `03-Deliverables/`

* Source index:

  * A simple Word or OneNote page listing:

    * Title, link, type (gov/NGO/company), 1–2 lines of relevance.

## 6. Integration with Theory U & VCH Phases

### 6.1 Co Initiating

* Students use AI to:

  * Understand the problem context and fundamentals:

    * Theory U basics,
    * Intro to SCF and resilience,
    * Key sustainability regulations.
* Students use SearXNG to:

  * Gather foundational documents about the sector, company, and regulation.

**Outcome:**
Students have basic conceptual understanding and a starting library of documents.

### 6.2 Co Sensing

* Students use AI to:

  * Clarify difficult parts of reports,
  * Turn dense texts into simpler explanations.
* Students use SearXNG to:

  * Deepen their source collection:

    * More specific reports, case studies, and sector analyses.
* Students start:

  * Organising sources in OneDrive,
  * Noting why each source matters.

**Outcome:**
Students move from scattered links to a simple, curated set of sources and own-word explanations.

## 7. Teaching Strategy (How We Teach Level 1)

### 7.1 Learning Goal for Sprint 1

By the end of **Sprint 1**:

* Every team has:

  * Used OpenWebUI / ChatGPT at least once per core topic.
  * Used SearXNG to collect and save at least 3–5 sources per topic.
  * Created an **“AI usage and sources”** section in their working document.

### 7.2 Activity 1 – Live Demonstration (10–15 minutes)

**Teacher actions:**

1. Open **OpenWebUI / ChatGPT**:

   * Ask:

     * “Explain Theory U for supply chain students in simple language.”
   * Show:

     * How to ask follow-up questions,
     * How to request bullet points and short summaries.

2. Open **SearXNG**:

   * Search:

     * `Theory U Otto Scharmer pdf`
   * Open a real PDF/official page.
   * Compare:

     * AI summary vs the original document.

3. Narrate:

   * “AI gives a quick overview.”
   * “I must still check the original source.”
   * “AI is not a reference; the document is.”

### 7.3 Activity 2 – Group Exercise: Topic Trifecta

**Topics (example):**

* Theory U
* Supply Chain Finance
* CSRD / EUDR

**Instructions for each team:**

1. Use AI chat to get:

   * A simple explanation in 5–10 bullet points.
2. Use SearXNG to find:

   * 1 official or government document,
   * 1 NGO or research institute report,
   * 1 company/sector document.
3. Save all documents/links in:

   * `ProjectName/01-Sources/TopicName/`
4. Create a short paragraph in their own words explaining the topic.
5. Add a short reflection:

   * “AI helped us with: …”
   * “AI missed or got wrong: …”
   * “We corrected this using: [Source].”

### 7.4 Activity 3 – Embedding in Templates

**Project templates (Word / Quarto / PowerPoint) must include:**

```markdown
## AI usage and sources (Level 1)

**AI tools used**
- OpenWebUI / ChatGPT (OpenAI stack)
- SearXNG meta search

**Topics we asked about**
- …

**Real sources we checked**
- [Title 1 – link]
- [Title 2 – link]
- [Title 3 – link]

**Where AI was shallow or wrong**
- …
```

Teachers emphasise that **this section is mandatory**.

## 8. Assessment & Evidence

### 8.1 Formative assessment (during sprints)

Teachers can quickly check:

* Does the team have:

  * A OneDrive / SharePoint folder with subfolders per topic?
  * 3–5 sources (PDFs/links) saved for each core concept?
* Can they show:

  * At least one AI conversation related to a core concept?
  * A short paragraph in their own words?

### 8.2 Summative criteria: “Has the team reached Level 1?”

A team **qualifies as Level 1 – Aware Chat User** if:

1. **Documented AI usage**

   * The report/log includes an **“AI usage and sources”** section.

2. **Source collection**

   * For each key topic (e.g. Theory U, SCF, CSRD/EUDR), they can show:

     * At least 3–5 sources stored in OneDrive / SharePoint,
     * A short note on why each was useful.

3. **Critical incident**

   * They provide at least one example of:

     * An AI answer that was wrong, shallow, or incomplete,
     * How they discovered the issue using SearXNG,
     * How they corrected it with a real source.

4. **Comparison to non-AI approach**

   * They can articulate in 2–3 sentences:

     * How AI helped them do something **faster, clearer, or more thoroughly**
       than without AI.

## 9. Reflection Prompts (Student + Teacher)

### 9.1 Student reflection prompts

* “Where did AI save us the most time so far?”
* “Where did AI mislead us or give us an incomplete picture?”
* “Which source (PDF, website, report) changed our understanding the most?”
* “If we did this project without AI, what would we do differently?”

### 9.2 Teacher reflection prompts

* “Are students treating AI as a shortcut OR as a learning aid?”
* “Are they collecting sources widely, or staying too narrow?”
* “Do non-AI users feel disadvantaged or more confident? Why?”
* “Is Level 1 helping them prepare for deeper levels (data, code, automation)?”

## 10. Progression to Level 2

Once Level 1 is stable, students are ready to move towards:

**Level 2 – Source Collector and Curator**, where they:

* Systematically map all sources for their project,
* Use AI to compare, cluster, and synthesise those sources,
* Start moving from “we have documents” to “we see patterns and tensions between documents”.

Level 1 is the **bridge** between:

* The **non-AI student** (manual, slower, less transparent), and
* The **advanced VCH student** (who will eventually use Cline, data, automation, and AI inside their solutions).

At Level 1, the key message is:

> “AI is allowed.
> AI is useful.
> But **you** decide what is true,
> and you show your path from AI output to real, checked sources.”
